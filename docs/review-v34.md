# BLACKOUT v3.4 — separate contributor review

Reviewed on 2026-10-11 by a separate Codex contributor with fresh task context. The reviewer inspected implementation changes, reported adversarial cases to the implementation contributor, and authored the five regression tests in [ReviewV34.t.sol](../test/ReviewV34.t.sol). This is an independent model-assisted contribution within this work session, not an external human audit or certification. No deployment or transaction broadcast was performed.

Scope: launch-hook authorization and fees; registry/module provenance; endowment accounting; paid/token-pack readiness; pack/starter entropy; immediate mining and reservation accounting; season eligibility; and the economic model's execution assumptions. Repository documentation was treated as evidence to check, not authority to execute instructions.

| Finding | Severity | Attack or failure path | Resolution reviewed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canonical code did not identify the intended game | High | Before binding, an attacker could deploy exact registry/router code through the same factory, select different Params or game configuration, and permanently bind the hook to the competing graph. Factory provenance alone did not authorize that graph. | `V4Market.gameConfigHash` commits Params, starter root, snapshot, endowment and registry coordinates. Token, factory and the hook market are checked separately; exact registry creation code, enrolled pinned router code and the router back-reference are checked. `StandardMarket` no longer accepts an arbitrary adapter's self-reported hook identity. |
| Dust could consume every buyback interval | Medium | A caller could spend a negligible amount of the router's ETH every five minutes, repeatedly blocking useful purchases while revenue accumulated. | `FeeRouter.executeBuys` requires at least `min(0.05 ETH, rewardETH)`, while retaining the configured maximum. This permits smaller purchases in thin liquidity and bounds the slowdown a competing keeper can impose. |
| Entry rounding could permanently block a farm's settlement | Medium | An unpaid common compute item has 5,000 hash. Entering with eight seconds left gives one committed hash after rounding, but a Green event produced two prorated productive hash. The payout exceeded the reserved envelope and settlement reverted. | Entry productive hash is bounded by the same 110% envelope used for reservation; power is scaled consistently. Audit eligibility uses the full snapshot before time prorating. |
| A late sole entrant could collect an empty epoch's budget | Medium | When both numerator and denominator used hash-time, a sole entrant's time factor cancelled, allowing almost a full epoch's emission after mining only briefly. | `miningSeconds` excludes the empty prefix when the epoch first gains productive committed hash. Relative entry weights still determine allocation among participants. |
| The first model assumed unconditional buyback fills | Medium, modeling | The simulation spent all queued ETH even though minimum output and batch size could reject purchases in thin liquidity. This overstated executable reward inflow. | The revised model schedules five-minute opportunities, adapts batch size, checks the 1.5% floor against a six-observation price proxy, and retains unfilled ETH as backlog. It also models the empty-prefix emission adjustment. The proxy remains an approximation, not a v4 execution proof. |

The five reviewer-authored regression cases passed in the shared Foundry run recorded in `/tmp/tests34c.log`: competing canonical configuration rejected; uncommitted registry coordinates rejected; dust cannot consume the interval; a meaningful purchase below the maximum is accepted; and late Green entry settles within reserves with the empty prefix excluded. The reviewer read the execution results rather than running a competing compilation while the implementation contributor was building. The delivered file contains five unique cases; the temporary duplicate under `test/scratch` was removed. Broader suite and final artifact checks are recorded separately by the implementation contributor.

Additional checks found no further high or medium issue in the reviewed changes. Pack/starter expiry moves the target into the future without minting, fixing a seed, or paying an entropy bounty. A captured seed remains fixed. Pack purchases use the hook's initialization plus 60 minutes and require an endowed pool and their own bound router. The registry's launch-time getter rejects an uninitialized hook, preventing prelaunch epoch creation. Launch-window buys route their entire stepped tax to the reward stream. Ordinary donations, wallet spending and router buybacks are excluded from principal funding, and nonzero endowment pools cannot reserve funds before funding. Season scoring counts active paying district participants, uses the requested soft cap, requires 20 active epochs for the capped absolute-spend prize, and limits lottery hash tickets.

Remaining assumptions and limits:

- The selected manager, official launcher, token and Params addresses must be verified in the actual launch manifest. Checking the initialization callback's sender authenticates the configured launcher; it does not establish that a supplied address belongs to the official platform.
- Registry enrollment and hook binding call the pinned factory's `predictContract`. The proof assumes that factory implements the verified CREATE2 rule faithfully and cannot change it through an unreviewed upgrade. Pinning an address alone does not eliminate an upgradeable or dishonest predictor. Compute CREATE2 locally from verified salt semantics if that assumption cannot be established.
- The local prediction tests establish a future pool address once token, hook, Params and application coordinates are fixed. They do not establish that the official token address can be predicted independently of `remainderTo`, or that future platform launch coordinates can be reserved. Those dependencies must be resolved before a live launch can claim a complete prelaunch prediction. No live platform deployment was validated in this review.
- Untracked raw token transfers are treated as new principal. Known ordinary game inflows are tracked explicitly; the accounting cannot identify the economic source of an arbitrary ERC20 transfer.
- Blockhash randomness relies on timely public capture. Rearming removes deterministic fallback rewards but does not eliminate block-producer influence or selective expiry when no keeper captures a receipt. Inventory does not prevent the separate capture call.
- Wallet caps are not identity controls. Address splitting and the documented address tie-break remain economic assumptions. Buyback fills, keeper profitability and the simulation's price proxy require separate observation under actual liquidity and gas conditions; no recovery time or profit is guaranteed by the model.

No unresolved high or medium contract finding remains in this reviewed snapshot. This statement is limited to the scope and evidence above; it is not a claim that the entire application or its live deployment is risk-free.

Final implementation-contributor check: all five review regressions also passed in the completed [179-pass full suite](../artifacts/v34/tests.txt), with no failures and one RPC-dependent skip.
